Cinematic direction: “The Living Digital Landscape”
The site should feel less like navigating pages and more like entering Marzia’s private digital universe. It is quiet, intelligent, responsive, and slightly mysterious. Nothing moves merely to decorate the screen—every particle, reflection, sound, and object reacts to the visitor or advances the story.
The experience follows this emotional arc:
Arrival → Recognition → Exploration → Understanding → Connection

1. The world and its visual rules
Color palette
The environment is almost black, but never pure black.
* Deep aubergine: #100B18 Main atmosphere and distant background.
* Midnight violet: #191126 Used to create depth around project planes.
* Porcelain lavender: #F4EEFA Main typography and illuminated project surfaces.
* Soft violet: #B793D2 Secondary text, borders, reflections, and atmospheric light.
* Electric botanical green: #A8C947 Particles, active states, movement paths, and living energy.
* Warm cursor gold: #E6B84A Used only for the cursor, focus points, and rare moments of human warmth.
* Reflection purple: #715A92 Water reflections and distant light columns.
Green should occupy less than 8% of the screen. Gold should occupy less than 1%. Their rarity gives them meaning.

2. Typography
The typography creates the tension between monumental expression and precise engineering.
Display type
Use a very tall, tightly spaced condensed grotesk for statements such as:
CRAFT, TASTE & CODE.
and:
SELECTED WORK
Possible choices:
* Druk Condensed, if licensed
* Barlow Condensed Black as an open-source alternative
* A customized condensed cut based on your existing type system
The display letters should feel architectural. They are not merely placed over the environment—they can pass behind particles, receive light, reflect subtly in the water, and dissolve at their edges.
Body type
Use Geist Sans, which is already in the project.
* Regular or 450 weight for descriptions
* 600 for project titles
* Tight but comfortable line height
* Slightly warm white rather than pure white
Technical labels
Use Geist Pixel Square sparingly for:
* Coordinates
* Project numbers
* Sound state
* Scroll instructions
* Years and roles
This creates a technical texture without turning the site into a science-fiction dashboard.
There should be only three typographic voices:
1. Monumental condensed display
2. Human Geist body copy
3. Pixel or mono microcopy

Full storyboard
Scene 0 — The arrival
Initial state
The screen begins almost completely dark.
There is no traditional loading percentage. Instead, three distant purple lights slowly appear along the horizon. They resemble lights across a black reflective landscape.
A quiet green particle enters from the left, followed by several more. They begin tracing an invisible path across the screen.
After approximately 1.2 seconds, the particles briefly form:
MARZIA
The word is imperfect and alive. Some particles fail to reach their position and continue drifting.
Loading sound
The arrival sound consists of:
* A very low, soft sub-frequency tone
* A distant metallic resonance
* A tiny electrical crackle as particles appear
* A muted pulse when loading completes
It should feel closer to hearing a machine wake in another room than hearing a dramatic movie trailer.
Sound begins muted. After the visitor’s first click, scroll, or pointer interaction, the site may gently offer:
SOUND OFF — ENABLE?
Never autoplay audible music without permission.

Scene 1 — Hero: Craft, Taste & Code
Entrance animation
The navigation fades in first. Each item appears through a short vertical blur:
* Marzia Saidi
* Index
* Work
* About
* Sound
* Availability
The large headline does not simply fade in.
Each line rises from below the reflective horizon like an enormous physical structure. The letters begin slightly compressed and stretch into their final proportions.
Sequence:
1. “CRAFT, TASTE” arrives.
2. Particles travel across its surface.
3. “& CODE.” rises 180 milliseconds later.
4. The final period lands with a quiet low-frequency pulse.
Total animation duration: approximately 1.8 seconds.
Project planes
Qalin, Relay, and SupportIQ exist at different depths.
They should never float randomly. They have physical weight:
* Qalin rests partially against the foreground.
* Relay is suspended farther back.
* SupportIQ appears more distant and slightly softer.
* Their angles respond subtly to perspective.
When the visitor moves the cursor, the planes rotate by only 1–3 degrees. Movement should feel heavy and cinematic, not like lightweight cards following the mouse.
Cursor
The default cursor becomes a thin gold ring with a tiny point at its center.
When resting:
* Diameter: approximately 48px
* Outer ring opacity: 60%
* Center point: solid warm gold
When approaching something interactive:
* The ring expands to 64–80px.
* The target label slightly moves toward it.
* The center dot remains precisely anchored to the actual pointer position.
The cursor must never lag so far that the interface feels inaccurate.

Particle behavior
The particles are the living nervous system of the website.
They should not move as one generic looping cloud. Their behavior changes according to the visitor and the current scene.
Default flow
At rest, particles follow a slow three-dimensional flow field:
* They enter from the left.
* Curve around typography and project planes.
* Descend near the foreground.
* Reflect faintly in the water.
* Disappear gradually at the edges.
The movement uses layered curl noise, creating natural turbulence without looking like smoke.
When the mouse approaches particles
There are three interaction zones around the pointer.
Outer zone: awareness
Within approximately 240px of the cursor:
* Particles begin turning toward the cursor’s movement direction.
* Their brightness rises slightly.
* The flow stretches into longer trails.
* Nearby particles accelerate by about 10%.
The particles appear to notice the visitor.
Middle zone: orbit
Within approximately 120px:
* Particles curve around the cursor.
* They form a loose elliptical orbit.
* Faster mouse movement produces a longer orbit.
* Slower movement allows particles to gather more closely.
This should feel magnetic, not like a simple repulsion effect.
Inner zone: contact
Within approximately 35px:
* Particles avoid the exact cursor center.
* They split around it like water around a stone.
* A few particles turn gold for 300–500 milliseconds.
* A very quiet granular sound responds to the amount of displacement.
If the visitor stops moving, particles slowly collect around the ring—but they never cover it completely.
If the visitor moves quickly, the cursor cuts a temporary path through the particle field. The opening repairs itself over approximately 1.4 seconds.
Near project planes
When the pointer approaches a project:
* Particles stop following the general environment.
* They stream toward the project’s edges.
* The project label becomes sharper.
* A thin particle outline briefly reveals the project’s perspective.
* The plane tilts slightly toward the visitor.
* The other projects lose 10–15% brightness.
On click, particles rush behind the plane, visually pulling the visitor into the case study.

The water
The surface is not meant to represent a literal ocean. It is a reflective computational floor—a visual device that gives the typography and projects physical presence.
Default behavior
The water is almost completely still.
It contains:
* Slow horizontal movement
* Very soft distorted reflections
* Fine surface grain
* Occasional circular ripples around distant purple lights
It should not look like a game-engine ocean with visible waves.
Cursor interaction
Only when the pointer moves through the lower third of the viewport does it affect the water.
The cursor creates:
* A subtle displacement beneath its position
* One small ripple after it stops
* A stretched reflection while moving quickly
* A faint gold highlight directly below the cursor
The ripple radius should stay under approximately 180px. The entire ocean should never react at once.
Scroll interaction
As the page scrolls:
* Reflections stretch vertically for a fraction of a second.
* The surface becomes smoother during transitions.
* When a new project locks into position, a single low ripple moves outward from its base.
The water provides weight. It tells the visitor that something has arrived or moved.

The purple lights
The shiny purple lights are navigation beacons embedded in the horizon.
They serve three purposes:
1. Establish scale and depth.
2. Illuminate project planes from below.
3. Communicate the current section.
They should not flash or pulse constantly.
Behavior by section
* In the hero, three lights glow faintly.
* As the visitor approaches Selected Work, additional lights appear.
* When a project becomes active, the closest light grows brighter.
* During transitions, the lights stretch into thin vertical beams.
* At the footer, the lights gradually shut down from left to right.
The lights also affect the environment:
* Project edges receive a lavender rim light.
* Reflections appear on the water.
* Nearby rocks become barely visible.
* Particles passing through the light change from green to pale lavender for a moment.
Sound connection: each light may correspond to one low musical note. Project changes create a very quiet chord, never a loud notification.

The rocks
The rocks are not fantasy-world decoration. They are physical anchors that prevent the floating project planes from feeling arbitrary.
They represent the solid part of your practice: craft and construction beneath the digital work.
Visual treatment
* Near-black angular surfaces
* Slight purple mineral reflection
* Tiny green particles caught in cracks
* No excessive detail
* No giant mountains
* No alien-organic forms
Only two or three rock groups should be visible in any viewport.
Behavior
Rocks remain mostly still because the scene needs visual stability.
They receive:
* Very subtle parallax
* Changing edge light as the pointer moves
* Water reflection
* Particle collisions
Particles should sometimes hit a rock edge and divide into two streams.
When the cursor approaches a rock, hidden particles in its surface briefly become visible, suggesting stored energy. The rock itself should not rotate or move.

Scene 2 — Transition into Selected Work
At the bottom of the hero, a pale diagonal plane is visible.
As the visitor scrolls:
1. The hero typography moves upward more slowly than expected.
2. The diagonal plane rises and temporarily covers the screen.
3. For approximately 400 milliseconds, the page becomes porcelain lavender.
4. A thin black line crosses the screen.
5. The plane rotates away in perspective.
6. The dark project landscape is revealed behind it.
This light interruption resets the eyes and separates introduction from exploration.
Sound:
* A soft reverse inhale
* A low paper-like sweep
* One restrained metallic click when the new scene locks into place

Scene 3 — Selected Work
This section is pinned while the visitor scrolls through the projects horizontally.
The page does not immediately move downward. Vertical scrolling advances the project corridor from left to right.
Section entrance
“SELECTED WORK” appears in the upper-left.
The letters initially contain faint image fragments from your projects. Over approximately one second, the fragments disappear and the letters become solid lavender-white.
Supporting text follows:
Products, systems and experiments— designed and built end to end.
The project planes emerge gradually from atmospheric darkness.
Project movement
Each project follows the same cinematic sequence:
Approaching
* The next plane becomes visible in the distance.
* Its label appears before the screen itself.
* Green particles begin traveling toward it.
* Its reflection slowly forms beneath it.
Active
* The plane moves to its primary composition.
* Perspective becomes clearer.
* The screenshot sharpens.
* The project number brightens.
* Role and year appear.
* A purple horizon light activates beneath it.
* Neighboring projects remain visible but quieter.
Leaving
* The plane rotates away by a few degrees.
* Its image loses saturation.
* Particles detach from its edge.
* Its reflection breaks apart before the panel disappears.
This keeps the projects connected as one continuous journey rather than eight separate cards.
Hover state
When a visitor hovers over the active project:
* The screen moves forward approximately 16–24px in virtual depth.
* Its dark border becomes slightly brighter.
* Particle density increases around the edges.
* “VIEW CASE STUDY ↗” appears from a masked vertical reveal.
* The screenshot itself moves 1–2% in the opposite direction from the plane, creating depth.
* A quiet focused tone replaces the environmental sound.
Click transition
When clicked:
1. The selected screen stops moving.
2. The cursor ring contracts into its center point.
3. Particles rush toward the four edges.
4. The plane expands until it fills the viewport.
5. The screenshot transforms into the case-study page.
6. The environmental sound filters out rather than abruptly stopping.
The transition should feel like entering the project, not opening another URL.

Scene 4 — Personal statement
After the final project, the environment becomes quieter.
The project planes disappear. Only particles and reflection remain.
Your text appears in two stages:
I like figuring out how things work, then finding ways to make them better.
Then:
I move between design, code, and AI to explore, prototype, and ship.
Animation
The first paragraph forms from stable white typography.
The second paragraph behaves differently:
* “design” is drawn by particles.
* “code” assembles in a precise mechanical sequence.
* “AI” appears as a moving light passing through the letters.
* “ship” creates one outward water ripple.
This section should contain no project screens and very few rocks. It is the calm scene where visitors understand how you think.
The sound becomes warmer:
* Less electrical texture
* A soft, sustained tonal layer
* Fewer high frequencies
* A gentle pulse matching the text reveals

Scene 5 — Footer and contact
The footer is the most minimal scene.
The headline appears:
LET’S CREATE THE UNEXPECTED.
The letters are enormous, but the environment behind them is quiet.
Headline behavior
“LET’S CREATE” remains solid.
“THE UNEXPECTED.” slowly dissolves from right to left into green particles. The word continually rebuilds itself, but the entire effect takes 8–12 seconds. It should not look like a quick glitch.
The particles travel from the dissolving letters to the contact plane on the right.
Contact plane
The contact interaction is a thin flat plane in perspective.
It contains:
START A CONVERSATION ↗
and a minimal waveform.
When hovered:
* The plane turns toward the visitor by approximately 2 degrees.
* Its border changes from lavender to green.
* Particles travel into the waveform.
* The waveform plays one short visual pulse.
* “EMAIL MARZIA” receives a gold underline.
When clicked:
* The surrounding environment darkens.
* The contact plane becomes flat and centered.
* The preferred email or contact experience opens.
* Sound resolves into one warm chord.
Final page ending
At the very bottom:
* The water becomes perfectly still.
* Particle production stops.
* Existing particles leave the screen.
* Purple lights shut down one at a time.
* Only the navigation baseline remains.
Footer information:
* Marzia Saidi © 2026
* Email
* LinkedIn
* GitHub
* Back to top
Hovering “Back to top” sends one green particle backward through the entire scene. Clicking it reverses the visual journey rapidly but smoothly.

Sound direction
The soundscape should be procedural and responsive rather than one looping song.
Main sound layers
1. Environmental bed
A quiet low-frequency atmosphere:
* Soft sub tone
* Distant air movement
* Very faint metallic resonance
* Almost imperceptible water texture
2. Particles
Particles produce delicate granular sounds.
* More particles near the cursor create slightly richer texture.
* Fast movement increases brightness, not volume.
* Gold particles produce a tiny glass-like accent.
* Particle sound must remain subtle enough that repeated cursor movement never becomes irritating.
3. Project movement
Project planes have physical audio weight:
* Approaching plane: filtered low sweep
* Locking into position: muted mechanical impact
* Hover: focused harmonic tone
* Opening: reverse suction followed by silence
4. Water
Water produces no constant splashing.
Only interaction creates sound:
* Cursor ripple: soft low droplet
* Project landing: deeper water resonance
* Section transition: long filtered wave
5. Purple lights
The horizon lights form a minimal musical system.
Each light represents a pitch. As sections change, they produce sparse chords. No melody should loop endlessly.
6. Footer
The sound progressively simplifies until only one pulse remains.
The final contact hover introduces the warmest sound in the experience—signaling that behind the futuristic world is a real human invitation.
Sound behavior requirements
* Sound begins off.
* The choice remains remembered during the session.
* The sound toggle clearly shows ON/OFF.
* Volume should be conservative.
* Every sound must have a visual equivalent.
* The experience must remain complete without sound.

Motion language
Use a small collection of consistent timing behaviors.
Interface elements
* Duration: 250–450ms
* Easing: cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)
* Used for navigation, labels, underlines, and cursor states
Heavy project planes
* Duration: 900–1400ms
* Easing: slow, weighted, slightly overshooting
* Used for entering, leaving, and perspective changes
Atmospheric motion
* Duration: 6–16 seconds
* Continuous but extremely subtle
* Used for particles, fog, reflection, and light breathing
Text reveals
* Duration: 700–1100ms
* Masked vertically rather than basic opacity fades
Nothing should bounce. Nothing should use playful spring animation. The world should feel heavy, controlled, and deliberate.

Reduced-motion version
Visitors who prefer reduced motion should still receive the same art direction.
* Project panels crossfade instead of moving through deep perspective.
* Particles become a slow ambient field.
* Cursor does not distort particles.
* Water remains static.
* Text does not dissolve.
* Case studies open with a short fade.
* Sound remains optional.
The experience should never require motion, sound, or precise pointer use to understand or navigate it.

The central directing principle
The future-facing quality should not come from adding more effects. It comes from making the entire environment behave as one connected system:
* The cursor affects particles.
* Particles reveal projects.
* Projects activate purple lights.
* Purple lights affect reflections.
* Reflections establish physical weight.
* Sound responds to the same events.
* Every scene gradually leads toward contact.
The visitor should feel that the site noticed them—and that their presence changed the world slightly.
